TakenRight

A medication companion that helps elders confirm what they've taken, resolve everyday doubts, and stay independent — while giving families clarity without turning care into monitoring.

How the idea started, and where it led

While doing secondary research for Clodoc, I noticed pharmacists play a role that goes beyond simply dispensing medicine. Around the same time, I watched an elder in my own family struggle with a simple but important question: had they already taken their medicine, or not?

It made me realize that elders living alone need more than reminders — they need certainty, and someone they trust when they're unsure.

That insight led to TakenRight: a medication platform connecting elders, caregivers, and pharmacists through one simple, supportive experience.

Understanding the problem

The challenge wasn’t only remembering to take medication. It was knowing whether it had already been taken. And solving doubts and uncertainty related to daily medication in elders living alone

Problem

  • May remember taking medication but later be unsure whether a dose was already taken.
  • Far living family is not sure about their parents' medication
  • Medication doubts remain unresolved

The gap

  • No support for clearing medication doubts
  • None are built around a trusted, regional-language pharmacist relationship — the part of this that isn't a reminder problem at all
  • Not elder friendly simple flow

How might we help an older person feel sure about their medication, without making them more dependent on family?

Research process & findings

01

Personal observation

Watched an elder in my own family second-guess a dose they'd probably already taken — and quietly let the doubt go rather than ask.

Secondary research

What I learned

01

Reminders aren't enough

The important moment isn't always before taking medication. It can happen afterward, when someone can't remember whether they took it.

02

Family is often the fallback

When unsure, an older adult may rely on their children for confirmation. But family members may not always be available.

03

Medication doubts need an easy answer

Knowing what a medicine is for can reduce uncertainty and help someone make sense of their medication routine.

04

Support should preserve independence

The goal isn't to make caregivers monitor every dose. It is to give older adults enough clarity to handle everyday decisions themselves.

Three principles shaped the product.

These principles became the filter for the product decisions that followed.

Clarity

Users should quickly understand what is due, completed or missed.

Simplicity

Medication is part of an existing routine, so interactions should require minimal effort.

Autonomy

Help should be available without making family members part of every medication decision.

Takeaways

People don't always need another reminder. Sometimes they need certainty after the reminder.

That changed the product from a simple reminder tool into a companion focused on:

  • Knowing what's taken.
  • Understanding the medication.
  • Getting help when unsure.
